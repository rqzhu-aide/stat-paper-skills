# Presentation Choices

Use this guide only when a passage has an unresolved problem of emphasis or explanatory depth. A named mode is not a prerequisite for writing, and clear sections need no classification. Different passages can need different treatments while retaining the manuscript's voice.

| Reader problem | Useful adjustment | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Familiar material is buried in setup, recaps, or repetition | Compress the setup and state the operative claim directly | Retain the target, reason for the construction, and material conditions. |
| An unfamiliar construction or conceptual jump arrives without a reason | Explain the paper-specific obstacle through a supplied contrast, decomposition, or diagnostic regime | Explain the research difficulty without reteaching familiar background. |
| Assumptions, objects, or dependencies are ambiguous | Clarify definitions, conditioning, scopes, and the stated result chain | Formal detail cannot replace an explanation of what the result means. |
| Results read as a catalogue or claims lack a visible comparison | Organize evidence around the statistical question, metric, comparison, and observed magnitude | Separate the reported pattern from an unverified explanation; retain the method and documented comparison basis. |

Choose the adjustment that resolves the passage's actual difficulty. A theorem may need one explanatory paragraph; a compact method section may need one detailed construction. Do not impose a uniform paragraph template or alternate treatments for variety.

## Explanations that retain the difficulty

Use supplied material that isolates the operative feature: a limiting regime, two nearby settings differing in one constraint, an error or objective decomposition, or a geometric or probabilistic interpretation. A numerical illustration helps when scale, sign, or threshold behavior is the question.

Do not use a toy example that removes the dependence, identifiability, high-dimensional, adaptive, or computational feature responsible for the research difficulty. Preserve that feature and distinguish exact relations from intuition. An equivalent explanation follows [support-and-author-decisions.md](support-and-author-decisions.md); a new scientific interpretation or example needs support before insertion.

Prefer an informative statistical contrast to an everyday analogy. If an analogy helps, make its mapping and limit explicit. Stop explaining once the intended reader can understand what the formal construction must resolve.
