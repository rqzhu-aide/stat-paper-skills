# Complete-Paper Writing Workflow

Use for drafting or structurally revising a complete paper. For a local edit, use its action and section guides directly. An audit diagnoses without following this drafting sequence.

## Read and plan

Read the supplied main text or research material, relevant supplement, displays, captions, and citation context before selecting an argument. Reuse the orientation source map. Establish article type, audience, supported central claim, venue/template, length convention, and main/supplement balance. Consult current official venue rules when formatting depends on them; distinguish submission pages from typeset pages and what counts toward a limit.

Keep one compact plan: what the reader knows entering each section and should understand leaving it, supported claims and source anchors, principal displays, approximate space, and unresolved scientific choices. Reuse an adequate plan. Use [argument-architecture.md](argument-architecture.md) for contribution positioning or competing narratives. Preserve supplied scientific choices; ordinary editorial decisions need no approval. Planning within a drafting request proceeds to writing.

Honor the requested architecture. Otherwise choose by intellectual need: theoretical result chain, methodology connecting construction and guarantees, or application organized around a scientific question. Distinguish the actual procedure, analytical representations, and stated guarantees; proof dependencies need not dictate the reader's introduction to the method. Allocate space by importance and difficulty, without paragraph, citation, or section quotas. For consequential unresolved disciplinary choices, use the targeted calibration in [wording-register.md](wording-register.md).

## Work focused sections

Work in a dependency-aware order using one active section guide. Read the short [prose core](polishing-protocol.md#prose-core-for-drafting-and-revision) and [wording-register.md](wording-register.md) once during complete-paper drafting and reuse them across sections; add guidance only for a distinct need. When drafting from research notes, preserve their scientific content while rewriting source-handling language and proof shorthand for the intended reader.

Before moving on, check whether the section delivers its intended understanding, with support, names, scope, paragraph progression, citations, and displays aligned. Update the plan only when a decision changes; resolve dependent passages together. Reuse settled work and calibration notes. Finalize the title and abstract after the body establishes its emphasis.

## Reconcile the whole paper

Read the assembled manuscript once as a continuous argument, using the source map rather than repeating all local checks:

- Revisit title, abstract, introduction, results, and ending after structural changes: align contribution, evidence strength, and boundary while retaining supplied hierarchy.
- Reconcile definitions, notation, assumptions, numbers, citations, cross-references, and main/supplement dependencies across sections and captions. Remove repeated setup while keeping distant passages understandable.
- Check whether successive paragraphs develop the argument or repeatedly classify its limits. Consolidate redundant qualifications while preserving scope at the relevant claim and where distant passages need it. Assess proportions, notation density, and display prominence by explanatory value, not just length. Not every result needs the same preamble or ending.
- Check credit at substantive use, including methods, comparators, data, and external interpretations. Reconcile figure/table labels with the statistical objects; captions should decode displays and body prose explain their contribution.
- Compile/render the complete editable manuscript when possible. Inspect page flow, heading hierarchy, overfull lines, awkward gaps or isolated headings, dense display sequences, floats, readable figure/table text at intended size, bibliography, and venue length. Respect submission placement rules rather than forcing every float immediately after its callout. Check applicable front/back matter using supplied information.

Repair consequential mismatches, then recheck changed spans and their dependencies; rerender when changes affect layout. Stop when the requested scope is coherent and remaining issues require new evidence or author choices. Report any unavailable rendering or material unresolved input. Do not create a full-audit harness or extra plan artifacts for ordinary writing.
